# Matematikk (MAT01-06)

LK20 sets matematikk goals after every trinn from 2. to 10., so this is the one
subject whose ladder resolves to a single school year. Ten strands carry the
progression. Each table row is Pensum's plain reading of the goals it cites; the
cited goals, in Udir's wording, stay the authority.

How to read a row: **the pupil can** is what a teacher should be able to see the
pupil do, on a table or on a screen. It is written to be observed, so it avoids
*understand* and *know*.

## 1. Counting and quantity

| trinn | the pupil can | LK20 |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | say how many up to 10 at a glance, without counting one by one, in dice and ten-frame patterns | KM13241 |
| 2 | count forwards and backwards from any number to 100, and in 2s, 5s and 10s | KM13230 |
| 2 | show a number as objects, a drawing, a numeral and a word, and match any two | KM13241 |
| 2 | sort a set by a property they choose, and sort it again a different way | KM13228 |
| 2 | tell odd from even by pairing objects, and say what is left over | KM13231 |

**Watch for:** counting objects twice or skipping one (one-to-one correspondence);
recounting from 1 when two groups are joined (not yet *counting on*); reading
"13" and "31" as the same.

**Activities**

- **Quick look** (`ten_frame`, P). A frame shows a pattern for as long as the
  pupil wants; the pupil taps "I know" and then builds the same pattern on an
  empty frame. Recognising structure is the skill, so the prompt is never
  hidden on a timer; the pupil hides it when ready.
- **Pairs and one left** (`counters`, C). Drag counters into pairs. If one is
  left alone the number is odd. The lonely counter gets a label, not a colour.
- **Same number, four ways** (`match`, P→A). Link dots, numeral, word and a
  base-ten picture that show the same number.
- *Mission:* count something at home in 2s (shoes, socks) and something in 5s.

## 2. Place value and the number line

| trinn | the pupil can | LK20 |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | build a two-digit number with tens and ones and say what each digit is worth | KM13232 |
| 2 | swap ten ones for one ten and back | KM13232 |
| 2 | place whole numbers to 100 on a line and use it to add and subtract | KM13233 |
| 6 | place decimals on a line and order numbers with different numbers of decimal places | KM13275 |
| 7 | place and compare negative numbers, and add and subtract with them on a line | KM13292 |
| 8 | write and compare powers of ten and square roots of perfect squares | KM13297 |

**Watch for:** zero read as "nothing" in 305; 0,25 judged larger than 0,3
because 25 > 3; −5 judged larger than −2.

**Activities**

- **Build it** (`base_ten`, C). *Show 34.* Accepts 2 tens and 14 ones while the
  skill is exchange, canonical only once it is not.
- **Break a ten** (`base_ten`, C). *You have 4 tens. Take away 17.* The pupil
  has to break a rod to do it. Regrouping is met as an action before a word.
- **Zoom line** (`number_line`, P). A line from 0 to 1 zooms into tenths and then
  hundredths when the pupil taps a segment. Placing 0,25 and 0,3 on the same
  zoomed line settles which is bigger without a rule.
- **Temperature line** (`number_line`, P, trinn 7). A vertical thermometer;
  *it is −3 °C and gets 5 degrees warmer*. Vertical because pupils already
  know thermometers go up.

## 3. Addition and subtraction

| trinn | the pupil can | LK20 |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | add and subtract within 20 using objects, a line or known facts, in a story from daily life | KM13234 |
| 2 | show that 3 + 5 and 5 + 3 are the same, and use it to count on from the bigger number | KM13235 |
| 3 | pick a strategy (count on, make ten, split tens and ones) and explain it | KM13243 |
| 3 | turn an addition into a subtraction and back (fact families) | KM13244 |
| 4 | explain how the four operations relate, with written and mental methods | KM13258 |
| 4 | estimate first, then check whether an answer is reasonable | KM13259 |

**Watch for:** subtracting the smaller digit from the larger in each column
(52 − 17 = 45); treating "more" in a story as always meaning add.

**Activities**

- **Make ten** (`ten_frame`, C). Two frames with 8 and 5. Move counters until
  one frame is full; the sum is read off 10 + 3.
- **Jump strategies** (`jumps`, P). *47 + 25.* Any jump path that lands on 72 is
  right. The evidence records which path, so a teacher sees who jumps in tens.
- **Part-whole bar** (`bar_model`, P). Stories where the unknown is the whole,
  a part, or the difference. The pupil drags the question mark to where the
  unknown is before any number is entered. Most errors in word problems are in
  this step, and it is untimed and unscored beyond "is the ? in the right place".
- **Estimate first** (`number_line`, P). Place where the answer will roughly be,
  then compute. The estimate is recorded and never marked wrong.

## 4. Multiplication and division

| trinn | the pupil can | LK20 |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | show multiplication as equal groups and as an array, and switch between them | KM13245, KM13247 |
| 3 | double and halve numbers to 100 in their head | KM13254 |
| 3 | turn an array round (4 × 6 = 6 × 4) and split one (4 × 6 = 4 × 5 + 4 × 1) | KM13248 |
| 3 | solve everyday problems that need × or ÷ | KM13246 |
| 4 | share into equal groups and group by a size, and say which one a story is | KM13255, KM13256 |
| 4 | use more than one written or mental method for division, and explain one | KM13257 |
| 8 | find the prime factors of a number and use them to simplify a fraction | KM13298 |
| 8 | use commutative, associative and distributive laws to calculate smartly | KM13303 |

**Watch for:** "multiplying always makes bigger"; confusing sharing (how many in
each group?) with grouping (how many groups?).

**Activities**

- **Stretch an array** (`array`, C). Drag a corner to make 3 × 4. Rotate it; the
  pupil sees it is still 12. Split it with a line into 3 × 2 and 3 × 2.
- **Share the apples** (`counters`, C). Deal counters one by one into plates
  (sharing), or fill bags of 4 (grouping). Same division, two actions; the
  story decides which.
- **Factor tree** (`sort` variant, P). Break a number into two factors, then
  break those, until nothing breaks. Primes are the leaves.

## 5. Fractions, decimals, percent

| trinn | the pupil can | LK20 |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | show that two different-looking pieces or amounts are the same size | KM13250 |
| 5 | show a fraction as part of a shape, part of a set, a division and a point on a line | KM13266, KM13267 |
| 5 | say which is bigger of two fractions and show why with strips | KM13268 |
| 5 | connect ½, 0,5 and 50 % as the same amount | KM13265 |
| 5 | solve a story problem with fractions | KM13269 |
| 6 | calculate with decimals, and compare the method to whole numbers | KM13276 |
| 6 | solve problems mixing fractions, decimals and percent, and explain the answer | KM13277 |
| 7 | switch between fraction, decimal and percent for any common value | KM13286, KM13288 |

**Watch for:** ¼ > ½ "because 4 > 2"; unequal parts counted as fractions;
adding numerators and denominators (½ + ⅓ = 2/5).

**Activities**

- **Strip wall** (`fraction_strips`, C). Stack thirds under a whole until they
  match; try to match a half with thirds and see it cannot be done. Equivalence
  is what fits.
- **Fair or not** (`sort`, P). Shapes cut into parts; sort into *equal parts*
  and *not equal parts*. Only the first pile has fractions.
- **Three names** (`match`, P→A). ½ ↔ 0,5 ↔ 50 % ↔ half a bar ↔ a point on a line.
- **Chocolate** (`bar_model`, C). The existing fraction figure, made
  interactive: cut the bar, shade what was eaten.

## 6. Equality, equations, algebra, functions

| trinn | the pupil can | LK20 |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | read = as "is the same as", and fill in 3 + 4 = ☐ + 5 | KM13242 |
| 3 | use =, < and > to compare amounts and expressions | KM13249 |
| 4 | write an expression for a situation, and a situation for an expression | KM13260 |
| 5 | solve a simple equation with objects or a drawing, and check the answer | KM13274 |
| 7 | solve equations and inequalities with more than one method, and check the answer | KM13291, KM13294, KM13296 |
| 7 | use the order of operations in a multi-step calculation | KM13287 |
| 8 | describe a pattern in words and then with a letter | KM13299, KM13301 |
| 8 | set up and solve equations and inequalities from a practical situation, and judge the answer | KM13304, KM13305 |
| 8 | show one relationship as a situation, a table, a graph and an expression | KM13302, KM13306 |
| 10 | solve a pair of equations from a real situation | KM13324 |
| 10 | read the slope of a line as change per unit, and tell linear from exponential growth | KM13320, KM13322, KM13319 |
| 10 | expand and factorise with the square theorems, and show them as areas | KM13318 |
| 10 | state a general rule algebraically, using the order of operations | KM13328 |

**Watch for:** = read as "the answer comes next" (3 + 4 = 7 + 5 = 12); a
letter read as an abbreviation (3a = "three apples") rather than a number.

**Activities**

- **Balance it** (`balance`, C→A). The through-line of the strand. At 2. trinn:
  numbers on pans. At 5.: a box whose weight is unknown. At 7.–8.: *remove the
  same from both sides* and *split both sides into equal groups* are buttons,
  and the written equation updates beside the pans each time. At 10.: two
  balances linked by the same boxes, which is a system of equations.
- **Pattern machine** (`match` + `coordinates`, P). A matchstick pattern grows;
  the pupil fills a table (step, sticks), then plots it, then picks the rule.
  Four representations of one function, in the order the goal lists them.
- **Square theorem tiles** (`array`, C). (a + b)² as a square split into a²,
  two ab rectangles and b². Drag the tiles to assemble it.

## 7. Shape and space

| trinn | the pupil can | LK20 |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | name, draw and sort common 2D shapes found around them, and say the rule they sorted by | KM13237 |
| 2 | spot, continue and make a repeating pattern | KM13236 |
| 3 | name and plot points in a coordinate system | KM13251, KM13252 |
| 4 | describe 2D and 3D shapes by angles, edges, corners and faces | KM13261 |
| 6 | say what two shapes have in common and what tells them apart | KM13278 |
| 6 | find and make lines of symmetry | KM13283 |
| 6 | move, flip and turn a shape, with and without coordinates | KM13284 |
| 9 | tell similar from congruent shapes, and use similarity to find a length | KM13308, KM13317 |
| 9 | use Pythagoras' theorem in a practical problem | KM13307, KM13317 |
| 9 | explain how changing one measurement in a problem changes the answer | KM13309 |

**Watch for:** a square turned 45° called "not a square"; only horizontal and
vertical mirror lines recognised.

**Activities**

- **Rubber bands** (`geoboard`, C). *Make a shape with 4 sides and no right
  angle.* Many answers are right; the evidence records which one.
- **Mirror** (`transform`, P). Complete half a picture across a line. The line
  can be diagonal.
- **Shape detective** (`shape_sort`, P). Bins labelled by property
  (*has a right angle*, *all sides equal*). A square goes in both, which is the
  point.
- **Pythagoras squares** (`geoboard`, C). Build squares on each side of a right
  triangle and count their areas.

## 8. Measurement

| trinn | the pupil can | LK20 |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | measure length and mass with hands, cubes, then cm and kg, and compare | KM13240 |
| 2 | read a calendar and an analogue and digital clock | KM13238 |
| 3 | measure area by covering with squares | KM13253 |
| 4 | measure volume with cubes and with litres | KM13264 |
| 5 | solve problems with time: durations, timetables | KM13273 |
| 6 | measure a circle and find that circumference ÷ diameter is always about 3,14 | KM13279 |
| 6 | find area and perimeter with more than one method, including splitting shapes | KM13280, KM13281, KM13285 |
| 9 | use and explain area, surface area and volume formulas | KM13310 |
| 9 | solve problems with compound units like km/h and kr/kg | KM13316 |

**Watch for:** measuring from 1 on a ruler instead of 0; area and perimeter
swapped; the minute hand read as hours.

**Activities**

- **Ruler** (`measure`, C). Drag a ruler under an object. The ruler does not
  snap to 0; lining it up is the skill.
- **Cover it** (`array`, C). Fill an irregular garden with square tiles; count
  tiles for area, walk the edge for perimeter. Same shape, two questions.
- **Clock hands** (`measure`, C). Set the clock to *half past three*. The hour
  hand moves with the minute hand, as on a real clock.
- **Roll the wheel** (`explore_sim`, P). Roll circles of different sizes one
  turn; the table of diameter and distance fills in; predict the next.

## 9. Data, statistics, chance

| trinn | the pupil can | LK20 |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | describe chance as impossible, unlikely, even, likely, certain, and connect it to fractions | KM13270 |
| 7 | collect, sort and present data in a table and a chart, and say why that chart | KM13290 |
| 7 | calculate mean, median and mode, and pick the one that fits | KM13289 |
| 9 | spot how a chart in the media can mislead | KM13312, KM13314 |
| 9 | calculate spread (range) and use it with averages to describe data | KM13313 |
| 9 | calculate probability in games and check it by simulation | KM13315, KM13311 |

**Watch for:** a bar chart whose axis does not start at zero read at face value;
"I rolled three sixes so a six is less likely now".

**Activities**

- **Class survey** (`tally_chart`, P). A given set of answers (favourite
  animal); tally, then build the chart. No real class data is collected.
- **Fix the chart** (`read_chart`, P). A misleading chart; tap what is wrong
  (the axis, the missing label).
- **Spin 100 times** (`trials`, P). Predict first, spin, compare. Repeat with
  1000. The prediction is locked before spinning.

## 10. Algorithms and programming

| trinn | the pupil can | LK20 |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | follow and give step-by-step instructions in a game | KM13239 |
| 3 | give instructions using a coordinate grid | KM13252 |
| 4 | write an algorithm with a repeat and a condition | KM13263 |
| 5 | program an algorithm with variables, conditions and loops | KM13272 |
| 6 | program a drawing of a shape or a pattern | KM13282 |
| 7 | use a program to explore a table of data | KM13293 |
| 8 | test and improve an algorithm | KM13300 |
| 10 | use a program to explore a mathematical property | KM13326 |
| 10 | read Python code and say what it does | KM13327 |

**Activities**

- **Robot path** (`step_code`, P). Arrow tiles move a character through a grid.
  At 4.: a *repeat 3* tile. At 5.: *if wall, turn*. At 6.: the path draws a
  shape. The primitive stays the same for eight years; only the tile set grows.
- **Blocks and text side by side** (`step_code`, A, trinn 8–10). The tile
  program and its Python equivalent are shown together; the pupil predicts
  where the character ends up from the text alone.
- **Bug hunt** (`step_code`, P). A program that almost works. Change one tile.

## 11. Money and modelling

| trinn | the pupil can | LK20 |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | use a spreadsheet to plan a small budget | KM13271 |
| 7 | check and explain a solution to a money problem | KM13295 |
| 10 | explain interest, loans and buying on credit, and their consequences | KM13321 |
| 10 | make a simple model of a situation and say where it stops being right | KM13323 |

Much of this strand is planning, presenting and judging, which a screen checks
badly. The on-screen part is `money` (making totals and change) and
`bar_model` (budgets as bars); the rest is missions: *plan a class trip for
500 kr*, *compare two phone plans*. KM13325 (an exploratory assignment) is
mission-only.

## Goals kept off screen

These are `not_assessable` for quizzes and are practised as missions:

- KM13229 *explore numbers through play, nature, art, music and literature*
- KM13262 *structures and patterns in games and play*: board games in class
- KM13325 *plan, carry out and present an exploratory assignment*
- the *assess and critically assess others' solutions* half of KM13259 and KM13295

## A sample run

Pupil in 3. trinn, strand 4, current skill *array turned round*; comfort
profile: calm, read aloud, theme *trains*.

| stone | task | primitive | stage |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | warm-up: *make 8 with counters*, a secure skill | `ten_frame` | C |
| 2 | *build 3 carriages with 4 seats*; how many seats? | `array` | C |
| 3 | turn the train round: 4 carriages with 3 seats. More, fewer, or the same? | `array` | C |
| 4 | a picture of 2 × 6 seats; pick the other way to write it | `multiple_choice` | P |
| 5 | 5 × 3 = ☐ × 5 | `numeric` | A |
| 6 | finish: pick *stretch an array* or *pairs and one left* | chosen | C |

If stone 5 is wrong, the next stone is the same question as an array to build
(stage steps down), and the run grows by one stone, announced before it
appears: *one more, then you are done*.
