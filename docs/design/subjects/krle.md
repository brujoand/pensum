# KRLE (RLE01-04)

Checkpoints after 4., 7. and 10. trinn, so a KRLE level is a three-year span.
The Education Act requires the subject to be taught objectively, critically and
pluralistically, and Pensum's rule follows from that: **items are graded only on
facts about traditions and on what a text says, never on belief.** *What do
Muslims celebrate at Eid al-Fitr* has an answer. *Is there a god* is not an
item.

**Watch for:** one tradition presented as uniform (all Christians, all
Buddhists believe the same); a festival confused with the tradition next to it
(Easter and Passover, Diwali and Hanukkah as "the light festival"); *ethics*
treated as *rules*.

## Activities

- **Festival year** (`sequence`, P). A year wheel. Place festivals in their
  season, graded up to the calendar's own variation (moving feasts get a range).
  Then `match` festival to tradition to symbol.
- **Which tradition** (`sort`, P). Objects, buildings and practices sorted by
  tradition. From 7. trinn, a Venn with *both* in the middle: prayer is in both
  Christianity and Islam, and that overlap is the lesson.
- **Story order** (`sequence`, P). Four panels from a central narrative.
- **Term cards** (`match`, P). Term to plain definition.
- **What kind of source** (`sort`, P). *Sacred text*, *scholar*, *a believer's
  own account*, *news*. From 10. trinn: what can each tell you, and what not.
- **Whose view** (`perspective`, P). A short dialogue between two characters
  from different traditions or none; which reason fits which person, from what
  the text says.
- **Dilemma ladder** (`perspective`, P, 7.–10.). A dilemma; sort arguments into
  *consequences*, *duty*, *character*. Classifying an argument is gradable;
  weighing it is not.

## Philosophy is a conversation

KM14590, KM14602 and KM14619 are conversations, and a screen should not stand
in for one. Pensum supplies the structure a teacher can use to make one
predictable:

- **Question card**: one open question, printed large.
- **Turn card**: *I think … because …*, *I agree with … because …*, *I see it
  differently because …*. Sentence starters make the rules of the conversation
  explicit, which helps pupils who find unwritten conversational rules hard.
- **Thinking ladder**: *what do we mean by …*, *give an example*, *give a
  counterexample*.

These are printable mission cards, with a teacher confirmation that the pupil
took part. Nothing about what the pupil said is recorded.
