# Norsk (NOR01-08)

Checkpoints after 2., 4., 7. and 10. trinn. Norsk is the subject every other
subject leans on, because a pupil who cannot yet read the prompt cannot show
what they know about fractions or photosynthesis. So two rules apply across
Pensum, not only here: **every prompt can be read aloud**, and **no activity
outside norsk tests reading by accident**.

About a third of the norsk goals are things a pupil does with other people
(*samtale*, *presentere*, *gi tilbakemelding*). Those are missions, listed at
the end. The rest have a screen activity.

**Watch for:**

- *Decoding:* guessing the word from the first letter and the picture instead of
  blending through it. The design never shows a picture that gives the word away
  during a decoding task, which is the figure rule in the README applied to
  reading.
- *Spelling:* the Norwegian-specific traps: double consonants (*takk*, *tak*),
  silent letters (*hv*, *gj*, *-dt*), *kj/skj*.
- *Grammar:* a word's class judged from its meaning alone (*løp* is a noun in
  *et løp*).
- *Comprehension:* answering from prior knowledge rather than from the text.
  Activities ask for the sentence that shows it, not only the answer.

## Activities

### Sounds and decoding (etter 2. trinn)

The Norwegian evidence here is the GraphoGame sequence (see
[landscape.md](../landscape.md)): regular, frequent sound–letter pairs first,
then blending, then less regular pairs. Pensum follows that order rather than
the alphabet.

- **Sound boxes** (`sound_boxes`, C). Hear *sol*; push a counter into a box for
  each sound (3); then drag letters into the boxes. Counting sounds before
  naming letters is the step that separates phonemic awareness from spelling.
- **Sound slide** (`blend`, P). Letter tiles *s*, *o*, *l* sit apart. Touching a
  tile says its sound. Sliding them together says them faster, until the word
  is said. The pupil taps the picture that matches.
- **Rhyme bins** (`sort`, P). Pictures, spoken on tap; sort into *rhymes with
  hus* and *does not*. No written words, so it tests hearing, not reading.
- **Letter tracing** (`trace`, exists). Kept as it is, with its stated limits.

### Reading comprehension

- **Find the proof** (`highlight`, P). A short text and a question. The pupil
  taps the sentence that answers it, then picks the answer. The tap is graded,
  which rewards reading the text over knowing the topic.
- **What happened first** (`sequence`, P). Four picture panels from a story the
  pupil has just read or heard. Order them.
- **Read aloud** (`read_aloud`, exists). Unchanged, including the stated tension
  between its stars and the recogniser's accuracy.

### Spelling and punctuation

- **Word workshop** (`word_build`, P). Build *fotball* from *fot* + *ball*; build
  *hoppet* from *hopp* + *et*. Compounds and inflections are the same action.
- **Listen and spell** (`listen_spell`, exists). Extend its distractor table
  with the double-consonant and silent-letter traps above.
- **Sentence carpenter** (`sentence_build`, P). Word tiles and punctuation
  tiles; build *Hvor bor du?* A capital letter is a tile flip, not a separate
  rule.

### Words and grammar

- **Word class hunt** (`highlight`, P). Tap every verb in a short passage. Tapped
  words get a marker shape (circle for verb, square for noun), never only a
  colour.
- **Sort by what it does** (`sort`, P). Words in sentences, sorted by class.
  The same word appears in two sentences as two classes.

### Writing texts

A screen cannot grade a text, and Pensum does not try. The on-screen part is
structure:

- **Story ladder** (`sequence`, P). Cards for *beginning*, *problem*, *what
  happens*, *ending*. The pupil orders them, then writes one sentence per card
  on paper or in a text field that is kept only in their browser.
- **Say it to someone** (mission). Read your text to someone, and write down one
  thing they asked about.

### Literature and figurative language

Figurative language is itself a curriculum goal (KM14171, KM14192), and for
many autistic pupils it is the part of norsk that has to be taught explicitly
rather than absorbed. So it is taught explicitly:

- **What it says, what it means** (`match`, P). *Hun har sommerfugler i magen* is
  drawn literally beside three meanings. Pick the meaning. The literal drawing is part
  of the lesson, not a joke at the pupil's expense.
- **How does she feel** (`perspective`, P). A short passage; which feeling fits
  the character, and which sentence shows it. Graded only when the text states
  or clearly shows it.

### Sources and critical reading

- **Fact or opinion** (`sort`, P). Sentences into *fact*, *opinion*, and, from
  7. trinn, *advertising*.
- **Can you trust it** (`highlight`, P). A made-up web page; tap what tells you
  who wrote it, when, and why.

### Language around us

- **Bokmål or nynorsk** (`sort`, P). *Ikke / ikkje*, *hvem / kven*. Sort, then
  match pairs.
- **Neighbour words** (`match`, P). Swedish and Danish words to Norwegian ones,
  including false friends (Swedish *rolig* means "fun"; in Norwegian and
  Danish it means "calm").

## Missions (goals a screen does not check)

| goal | mission |
|---|---|
| KM14151, KM14166 library | Borrow a book you chose. Write its title in your reading log. |
| KM14141, KM14158 creative response | Draw, act or build something from the story. Show it to someone. |
| KM14142, KM14167, KM14169 conversation | Talk with a partner using the turn card: listen, repeat what they said, add one thing. |
| KM14154, KM14170 presenting | Present for one minute from three picture cards. |
| KM14174 peer feedback | Give a classmate one "this works" and one "try this", using the feedback card. |
| KM14149, KM14160 language affects others | Find two ways to say the same thing to a friend and to the headteacher. |
| KM14165, KM14178 dialects | Ask an adult how they say three words, and write them down. |
| KM14155, KM14183, KM14189 multimedia texts | Make a poster that combines picture and text. |
| KM14182, KM14199 digital media | Classroom discussion; not practised in Pensum. |

The *turn card* and *feedback card* are printable, fixed-format scripts. A
conversation with a written structure is predictable, which is what makes it
possible for pupils who find open conversation hard.
