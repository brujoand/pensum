# Engelsk (ENG01-06)

Checkpoints after 2., 4., 7. and 10. trinn. English reuses most of the norsk
primitives (`blend`, `sound_boxes`, `listen_spell`, `sentence_build`,
`highlight`), which means a pupil meets a familiar interaction with new
content. The primitive specific to English is `dialogue`, because LK20 asks for
rehearsed conversations from 2. trinn (KM13333).

**Watch for (Norwegian first-language transfer):**

- **Verb second.** *Yesterday played I football* follows Norwegian word order.
  The most common structural error, and a `sentence_build` task makes it
  visible.
- **do-support.** *Like you pizza?* for *Do you like pizza?*
- **False friends.** *eventually* (not *eventuelt*), *become* (not *bli*, which
  is *get* or *be*), *actually* (not *aktuelt*).
- **Sounds Norwegian lacks.** *th* (/θ/, /ð/), *w* against *v*, and *z*.

## Activities

- **English sound slide** (`blend`, P). The norsk primitive with English
  grapheme–phoneme pairs, so *sh*, *ch* and *th* are single tiles.
- **Picture dictionary** (`match`, P). Word to picture, with audio on tap.
  Words common to both languages (*hand*, *finger*) come first because they
  succeed; that is the warm-up.
- **Word order train** (`sentence_build`, P). Carriages with words; build the
  sentence. The V2 trap is targeted directly: *Yesterday / I / played /
  football*. A wrong order is shown back as it would sound, then corrected
  with the time word moved to the front of the train.
- **At the café** (`dialogue`, P). A scripted conversation with an animated,
  calm character. The pupil picks each line from three. Every acceptable line
  leads on; an impolite one gets a literal reply (*The waiter did not
  understand. Try "Could I have…"*). The script is the same every time until
  the pupil has finished it once. After that the character varies its lines,
  which is the move from rehearsed to spontaneous conversation that LK20
  describes.
- **Guess from context** (`highlight`, P). A sentence with one unknown word;
  tap the words that helped you guess it, then pick its meaning.
- **False friend sort** (`sort`, P). *Means the same in Norwegian* against
  *means something else*.
- **Listen and spell** (`listen_spell`, exists). The English table already
  exists; extend it with the *th/f/d* and double-letter confusions.

## Conversation and anxiety

Speaking a second language in front of others is the most anxiety-heavy task
in the subject. Pensum keeps speaking on screen private: `dialogue` is picked,
not spoken, and a spoken version (reusing the read-aloud recogniser) is opt-in
and never scored, because the README's own limits section records that the
recogniser mishears children and second-language speakers most.
