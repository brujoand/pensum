# What already exists

A survey of tools children and teachers already use, read for one question each:
what should Pensum take, and what should it avoid. Gathered by web search in
September 2026. Vendor claims (user counts, effect sizes from vendor-funded
studies) are marked as such. Products that could not be verified from an
independent source are listed at the end, not described.

## Mathematics

| product | core interaction | game layer | teacher view | take | avoid |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| DragonBox (Kahoot) | drag picture tiles; symbols appear only once the idea is there | stars per level, no ranking | none | concept before notation | teachers see nothing |
| Polypad (Mathigon) | free virtual manipulatives: tiles, fraction bars, geometry | none | shared canvases | manipulatives first; free, no login | no progression or sequencing |
| ST Math (MIND) | language-free visual puzzles before any words or symbols | game-based | yes, US standards | a way in that needs no reading | closed district licensing |
| NumBots | short addition/subtraction fluency levels | robot avatar, coins, stars | yes | light, non-competitive loop over sound pedagogy | ceiling at early number |
| Khan Academy (Kids) | tap and type, hints, retries | mastery points, not speed | yes | mastery progress free of any clock | no LK20 |
| Zearn | video lesson, then practice on screen and paper | light | flags pupils who keep missing | tells the teacher who is stuck, in real time | US only |
| Matific | short activities | light | per-outcome reports | reporting at outcome grain | the teacher assigns everything by hand |
| Prodigy | RPG battles won by typing answers | pets, gear, subscription tiers | yes | a story makes pupils choose to practise | upselling to children; procedural depth only [1] |
| Brilliant | guided problem steps | streaks | none | intuition-first steps | built for self-starters, not strugglers |
| Kikora (NO) | calculation checked step by step | optional inter-school competition | full working per pupil | feedback on each step, not only the answer | the competition mode |
| Multi Smart Øving (NO, Gyldendal) | adaptive drill, 1.–7. trinn | light | daily class and pupil view | Norwegian adaptive practice with independent research [2] | tied to buying the textbook |
| Campus Inkrement (NO) | flipped: video, tasks, tests | light | full data overview | LK20-aligned and used widely | needs the lesson restructured round it |

## Classroom tools

| product | take | avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Kahoot | low-stakes review as a game | countdown plus a visible leaderboard: rewards speed, shows lower-ranked pupils their rank [6] |
| Blooket, Gimkit | varied skins keep it new | quizzing fitted into a game rather than a game serving the learning; currency and upgrades |
| Quizlet | spaced repetition as a basic mechanism | core pedagogy behind a paywall; ads |
| Seesaw | portfolio visible to teachers and parents instead of a ranking | not a practice engine |

## What this adds up to

**Patterns to adopt**

1. Concept before notation (DragonBox, ST Math) → CRA stages on every skill.
2. Manipulatives you can play with before being scored (Polypad, Tinybop) →
   *Check is a button*; undo is free.
3. Feedback on the step, not only the answer (Kikora) → evidence records the
   path; hint step 2 shows what was built.
4. Teacher view answers "who is stuck, on what" (Zearn, Multi Smart Øving) →
   the class grid highlights *practising, concrete only*.
5. Rewards tied to completion and mastery, never to speed (NumBots, Khan) →
   the map and collections.
6. Calm and predictable as a stated goal (Endless, Choiceworks) → calm on by
   default.
7. A way in that needs little reading (ST Math) → read-aloud and picture-first
   prompts.
8. Norwegian evidence exists and should be used: GraphoGame's sequence for
   early decoding, ALMAT's research on adaptive practice [2][3].
9. Free, with no ads and no upsell (PhET, Zearn, Teach Your Monster) → already
   Pensum's stance.

**Patterns to refuse**

1. Countdowns and visible leaderboards (Kahoot, Blooket, Gimkit).
2. Upsell or paywalled pedagogy aimed at children (Prodigy, Quizlet).
3. A game bolted onto a quiz, where the game and the learning are separate
   (Blooket, Classcraft).
4. Streak and league psychology (Duolingo's main app).
5. An in-app metric presented as real-world progress (EndeavorRx). Pensum's
   *secure* means secure on these tasks, and the teacher view says so.

## The gap Pensum fills

No product found combines all three of:

1. **LK20 progression at goal level, openly readable.** Norwegian tools that
   are curriculum-precise (Kikora, Multi Smart Øving, Campus Inkrement) are
   closed, publisher-owned, and mostly single-subject.
2. **A calm, tactile-first interaction model.** The tools designed around
   calm and predictability (Endless, Choiceworks, Toca Boca) are not
   curriculum-mapped and measure little.
3. **Mastery visible to both teacher and pupil across subjects**, without
   ranking pupils against each other.

The design also has to take a position the ADHD gamification literature mostly
avoids: frequent small rewards help attention, and the same variable-reward
mechanics carry a compulsion risk and overstimulate other pupils. Pensum's
answer is frequent **and** predictable: every reward is earned deterministically
and explained, and none is random (see [principles.md](principles.md#what-gamification-is-allowed-to-do)).
